# Single-reviewer human validation protocol

## 1. Purpose and limits

The project already compares two deterministic rule-based procedures (Coder A and Coder B). Their Cohen's kappa, bootstrap confidence intervals and raw agreement are **inter-method agreement**. They do not constitute human validation.

This protocol is designed for a study in which **one human reviewer** can inspect the official source documents. It provides a transparent, source-based audit of a reproducible sample and a comparison between the reviewer's scores and the automated scores.

A single reviewer cannot estimate human-human inter-rater reliability. Do not calculate or report human-human Cohen's kappa, do not describe the review as double coding, and do not call the reviewer's scores an independently established gold standard. Use the terms **single-reviewer validation**, **human review**, **human–algorithm concordance**, and **reviewer-assessed reference coding**.

The exercise improves source verification and provides evidence about the plausibility of the automated classifications. It does not eliminate the possibility of reviewer error or subjective interpretation.

## 2. Sample

Use the reproducible sample of up to 60 policy instruments generated by `create_single_reviewer_template()` in `src/single_reviewer_validation.py`. It uses the project's existing sampling function: policies are sampled by the provisional document-level remedy category (0–3) where possible, and a seeded random top-up is used to reach the target size.

Record the actual sample size and the number selected from each remedy stratum. Because this is a stratified audit sample rather than a simple random sample, do not present unweighted sample proportions as unbiased estimates of error rates for all 244 policies. If you report an overall concordance percentage, label it as applying to the reviewed sample. For population-level estimates, use stratum weights and explain the design.

The sample is selected from the provisional minimum-of-A-and-B coding. This is a transparent review-sampling decision, not evidence that the minimum rule is correct.

## 3. Blinding and review sequence

1. Generate `human_validation_single_reviewer_template.csv` after the notebook has created the policy-level `pol` data frame.
2. Keep A/B and reconciled scores out of the review worksheet while coding. The template intentionally omits those scores to reduce confirmation bias.
3. Review the official source document for each policy. Prefer the official gazette, legislature, regulator, ministry or other issuing body's source. A tracker summary, news report or third-party commentary alone is not sufficient evidence for a legal-status decision.
4. Record the official document title, issuing body, URL, source type, accessibility and date checked.
5. Code the document-level remedy score, any-stakeholder inclusion and (where feasible) each of the five stakeholder-group scores using the codebook below.
6. Record the exact article/section/page and a short quotation or precise paraphrase supporting every non-zero score. For a zero score, note which source sections/search terms were checked and whether the source was complete and accessible. A missing or inaccessible source must be marked as such, not coded as zero.
7. Only after all human scores are completed, join the worksheet to the A/B data and calculate human–algorithm comparisons.

## 4. Coding rules

### 4.1 Document-level remedy (0–3)

| Score | Label | Human-review rule |
|---:|---|---|
| 0 | No relevant mechanism identified | No qualifying symbolic, procedural or explicit individual remedy mechanism was found in the reviewed official text. Use only when the source was sufficiently complete and accessible. |
| 1 | Symbolic / aspirational | Principles, encouragement, voluntary commitments, ethical aspirations, recommendations or guidance without a qualifying procedural mechanism. |
| 2 | Procedural | A notice, explanation, complaint, appeal, contestation, human review/intervention, objection, opt-out or review procedure is specified. |
| 3 | Explicit individual right/remedy | The text establishes an explicit individual right to compensation, damages, redress, legal/judicial remedy or a right of action. |

Use the highest qualifying level found in the official instrument. A public regulator's power to impose a fine or sanction is **public enforcement**, not automatically an individual remedy. Do not assign score 3 solely because a regulator can fine an organisation.

### 4.2 Any-stakeholder inclusion (0/1)

- `1`: at least one of the five stakeholder groups in the project's codebook is explicitly identified in the official instrument or relevant official provision.
- `0`: none of the five groups is identified after review of a complete, accessible source.

This binary variable concerns the five coded groups, not every possible actor or duty-holder. Record source limitations where a source is partial.

### 4.3 Group-specific scores (0–3)

For consumers/individuals, workers, children, vulnerable groups and civil society, apply the project's group-level scale. A group receives a score of 2 or 3 only when the group and the qualifying safeguard/right are explicitly linked in the same sentence or provision. A general remedy elsewhere in the document is not automatically linked to every stakeholder group.

| Score | Meaning |
|---:|---|
| 0 | Group not identified in the reviewed text |
| 1 | Group mentioned without a linked procedural or explicit individual right/remedy |
| 2 | Group linked to a procedural safeguard |
| 3 | Group linked to an explicit individual right/remedy |

### 4.4 Legal and instrument classification checks

Record the official-source assessment of:

- legal status: enacted/in force, enacted/not yet in force, bill/draft, soft-law/policy guidance, repealed/superseded, or unclear;
- instrument type: act/statute, regulation, bill, executive order, strategy, policy, guidance/framework/standard/code, consultation, or other;
- current status: in force, not yet in force, draft, repealed, superseded, or unknown.

Do not silently overwrite tracker metadata. Preserve the tracker classification and record the reviewer's official-source assessment separately. Note the article, commencement provision, enactment date or official notice supporting a changed classification.

## 5. Missing, inaccessible and ambiguous sources

Use the source-access field to distinguish `accessible`, `partially accessible`, `inaccessible`, and `not found`. Do not convert missing source text into a zero score. Leave the substantive score blank and explain the limitation. If the source is accessible but genuinely contains no qualifying evidence after review, a zero may be assigned.

Where a document is long, use the table of contents and search within the official PDF/HTML, then inspect the relevant provisions in context. Do not rely on a search-result snippet alone. If a policy has multiple official versions, record which version/date was reviewed.

## 6. Analysis after review

Use `compare_human_with_automated()` from `src/single_reviewer_validation.py` to compare the completed human scores with:

- Coder A;
- Coder B;
- the provisional minimum-of-A-and-B score.

For remedy (ordinal 0–3), report the number of paired cases, exact agreement, quadratic weighted Cohen's kappa, mean absolute category difference and proportion within one category. For inclusion (binary 0/1), report paired n, exact agreement and unweighted Cohen's kappa. Include confusion matrices so readers can see the direction of disagreements.

These are **human–algorithm concordance** statistics. They are not human-human reliability estimates. Because there is one reviewer, no human-human kappa is available. Kappa can also be unstable or undefined in a small sample or where one category dominates; report the raw agreement and confusion matrix alongside it and explain undefined estimates.

The sample is stratified. If presenting an overall error/concordance estimate intended to represent the full 244-policy corpus, calculate design weights based on each stratum's population size divided by its sample size. Otherwise, explicitly limit the result to the reviewed sample.

## 7. Applying reviewer-assessed overrides

After review and comparison, create `human_review_overrides.csv` using `create_review_override_file()`. The current notebook's optional override hook expects the legacy column names `slug`, `adjudicated_remedy`, and `adjudicated_inclusion` in a file named `human_adjudication.csv`. For compatibility only, copy/rename the generated override file to that name and place it in the notebook working directory or `aipolicy_results/` before rerunning the downstream analysis.

Despite the legacy filename and column names, these values are **single-reviewer-assessed overrides**, not multi-reviewer adjudications. State this accurately in the paper. The current override hook updates document-level remedy and inclusion only; it does not replace group-specific scores, legal-status metadata or all accountability variables. Do not imply that all outputs have been human-corrected.

Retain the original A/B scores and provisional minimum scores for transparency. Report results before and after reviewer-assessed overrides as a sensitivity comparison where feasible.

## 8. Reporting template

Report, at minimum:

- number of sampled and successfully reviewed instruments;
- sampling method, seed and counts in each remedy stratum;
- number and share of official sources accessible, partial, inaccessible or not found;
- human–Coder A, human–Coder B and human–minimum(A,B) concordance for remedy and inclusion;
- confusion matrices and category-specific disagreements;
- number of tracker legal-status/instrument classifications confirmed, corrected or unresolved;
- limitations of a single reviewer, the sample design and source availability.

Suggested manuscript wording (fill in the brackets only after completing the exercise):

> A single-reviewer source-validation exercise was conducted on a reproducible stratified sample of [n] policy instruments, selected by the provisional document-level remedy category. The reviewer independently examined the corresponding official instruments and recorded document-level remedy strength, stakeholder inclusion, group-linked safeguards and official instrument classifications before inspecting the automated coder outputs. Human–algorithm concordance was assessed using [metrics]. This procedure provides a source-based audit but does not estimate human inter-rater reliability; all findings are therefore interpreted as single-reviewer validation rather than multi-coder adjudication.

Do not insert numerical results until the worksheet is completed and analysed.
